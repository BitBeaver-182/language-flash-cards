## Grammar and progression

### G01 · Alphabet and pronunciation

Revisited or practiced; teaching order may be uncertain. Letters and sounds are learned together. Hungarian digraphs (cs, dz, gy, ly, ny, sz, ty, zs) and the trigraph dzs are alphabetic units. Preserve vowel length: a/á, e/é, i/í, o/ó, ö/ő, u/ú, ü/ű. The Spanish comparison sheets are approximations, not exact phonetic equivalences.

The alphabet sheet, in order: a, á, b, c, cs, d, dz, dzs, e, é, f, g, gy, h, i, í, j, k, l, ly, m, n, ny, o, ó, ö, ő, p, q, r, s, sz, t, ty, u, ú, ü, ű, v, w, x, y, z, zs. Q, w, x and y occur in the foreign-letter portion. Important source sound comparisons: s ≈ English sh; sz ≈ English s; c ≈ ts; cs ≈ ch; ny ≈ Spanish ñ; j and ly share a sound. These are learning aids, not a complete phonetic transcription.

Source contrasts: kar/kár, kor/kór, örök/őrök. Stress practice in S131 puts stress on the first syllable. S131 also contains invented syllables and doubtful examples; see Review issues.

### G02 · Vowel harmony

Earliest explicit table in this sequence. Front vowels: e, é, i, í, ö, ő, ü, ű. Back vowels: a, á, o, ó, u, ú. Lessons distinguish front, back and mixed stems; most two-way suffixes choose the back form for mixed stems. Learn lexical exceptions rather than extending this teaching shortcut to every word.

házban; dzsipben; kávéban. Three-way endings distinguish rounded front vowels in examples such as örülök.

### G03 · Articles a az and egy

Earliest explicit table in this sequence. Use a before a consonant and az before a vowel. Egy introduces new information or a quantity. A/az can refer back to something known and normally precedes possessed nouns. Profession predicates are taught without an article.

Van egy macska a kertben. A macska fekete. Ildikó tanárnő. Ez egy drága autó / Ez drága autó are both shown in the teaching sheet.

### G04 · Personal pronouns and formal address

Earliest explicit table in this sequence. én I; te you singular informal; ő he/she/it; mi we; ti you plural informal; ők they. Ön and Önök address one or more people formally and take third-person verb forms. Unstressed subject pronouns are often omitted; retain them for contrast.

Japánul tanulok. Én tanulok japánul, nem Éva. Ő él Berlinben, nem Péter. The workbook gloss of ő as only masculine is incomplete.

### G05 · Present copula identity location and wellbeing

Earliest explicit table in this sequence. Identity/quality: vagyok, vagy, zero in third-person singular, vagyunk, vagytok, zero in third-person plural. Location or adverbial wellbeing retains third-person van/vannak. Adjectival feelings omit third-person van.

Ő mérnök. Ők magyarok. Csaba az irodában van. Ő jól van. Ő fáradt. Adjectives versus adverbs are explicitly distinguished: boldog versus jól/remekül.

### G11 · Numbers age and prices

Earliest explicit table in this sequence. Numbers are used for ages, prices, telephone numbers and quantities. Preserve the taught húsz → huszon- and tíz → tizen- combinations. Large numbers and forint/euro prices are practiced; historical price tags are lesson prompts.

Hány éves vagy? 33 éves vagyok. Mennyibe kerül? 320 forint. Learned forms include nulla, egy, kettő/két, három, tíz, húsz, harminc, száz, ezer, millió, milliárd.
