## g01

**Alphabet and pronunciation**

Letters and sounds are learned together. Hungarian digraphs (cs, dz, gy, ly, ny, sz, ty, zs) and the trigraph dzs are alphabetic units. Preserve vowel length: a/á, e/é, i/í, o/ó, ö/ő, u/ú, ü/ű. The Spanish comparison sheets are approximations, not exact phonetic equivalences.

The alphabet sheet, in order: a, á, b, c, cs, d, dz, dzs, e, é, f, g, gy, h, i, í, j, k, l, ly, m, n, ny, o, ó, ö, ő, p, q, r, s, sz, t, ty, u, ú, ü, ű, v, w, x, y, z, zs. Q, w, x and y occur in the foreign-letter portion. Important source sound comparisons: s ≈ English sh; sz ≈ English s; c ≈ ts; cs ≈ ch; ny ≈ Spanish ñ; j and ly share a sound. These are learning aids, not a complete phonetic transcription.

Source contrasts: kar/kár, kor/kór, örök/őrök. Stress practice in S131 puts stress on the first syllable. S131 also contains invented syllables and doubtful examples; see Review issues.

## g02

**Vowel harmony**

Front vowels: e, é, i, í, ö, ő, ü, ű. Back vowels: a, á, o, ó, u, ú. Lessons distinguish front, back and mixed stems; most two-way suffixes choose the back form for mixed stems. Learn lexical exceptions rather than extending this teaching shortcut to every word.

házban; dzsipben; kávéban. Three-way endings distinguish rounded front vowels in examples such as örülök.

## g03

**Articles a az and egy**

Use a before a consonant and az before a vowel. Egy introduces new information or a quantity. A/az can refer back to something known and normally precedes possessed nouns. Profession predicates are taught without an article.

Van egy macska a kertben. A macska fekete. Ildikó tanárnő. Ez egy drága autó / Ez drága autó are both shown in the teaching sheet.

## g04

**Personal pronouns and formal address**

## g09

**Residence suffixes and origin adjectives**

Residence uses -ban/-ben or -n/-on/-en/-ön according to the learned place name. The source teaches -ban/-ben for continents, most countries and many foreign towns; Magyarországon is an explicit exception. Many Hungarian towns and islands use surface endings. Origin adjectives use -i, with learned spelling changes.

Berlinben, Párizsban, Európában; Budapesten, Szegeden, Miskolcon, Máltán. Final a/e lengthens before the taught endings: Málta → Máltán, Lipcse → Lipcsében. London → londoni; Róma → római; Ausztrália → ausztrál.

## g10

## g13

## g15

When a noun takes several endings, the taught order is noun + possessive suffix + case suffix. A possessive stem may change before a later suffix.

## g16

**Nested possession**

The possessed noun can itself be the possessor of another noun. The final possessed item takes the third-person possessed form even when an earlier possessor is plural.

a nővérem széke; a főnököm irodája; a titkárnőtök laptopja; a városunk parkja. Compare the independent possessive ending on nővérem with the ending on széke.

## g17

**Negation nem nincs and hanem**

Whole-clause verbal negation places nem before the verb. Third-person existential/locative van becomes nincs/nincsen and vannak becomes nincsenek. Identity/adjective predicates use nem without inserting van. Contrastive negation places nem before the negated phrase and may continue with hanem.

Nem beszélek magyarul. Nem vagyok otthon. A tanár nincs a szobában. A diákok nincsenek az iskolában. Nem Péter tanul Rómában, hanem Géza. Nem otthon van, hanem az irodában.

## g19
