# Review issues and processing limits

This is an evidence-preserving study edition. The 2025 sequence has an explicit year; the other dated sequence has month/day only. K/U identifiers are generated catalogue IDs, not original lesson numbers. No original lesson numbers were found. Undated reference material remains undated. An encounter means the item appears in retained text or an extracted image; it does not prove that the rule was first taught that day.

All 581 distinct source images were visually inspected in labelled contact sheets. Their educational descriptions and the consolidated grammar tables reflect that review. Image OCR is a provisional search aid: Hungarian was unavailable as a local OCR language, so English/Spanish recognition was used. Fine print, table cell alignment, handwriting and diacritics are not fully proofread. Keep the original image alongside any uncertain extraction. Eight MP3 files and speech in three MP4 files are NOT TRANSCRIBED; sampled video frames were inspected and showed speakers without lesson text. Written listening exercises are preserved, but their answers were not checked against the recordings.

## Spelling and meaning

The source evidence reproduces original wording, including mistakes and learner answers. The dictionary normalizes selected headwords and distinguishes broader meanings; these are editorial clarifications, not changes to an exercise. Corrections include énekél → énekel, fiu → fiú, inteligens → intelligens, éredes → érdekes, elődás → előadás, kitünően → kitűnően and fűvészkert → füvészkert. Inflected forms such as tojások and kulcsok are stored under their singular headwords. Source variants remain traceable.

Flagged glosses: nagy means big, not very (nagyon); délelőtt is before noon, not afternoon (délután); túl means too rather than so; vicc is joke; antik is antique; karcol is scratch. The workbook's ő includes he/she/it, not only he. Hét has seven/week; vár has wait/castle; dél has noon/south. Family cousin/niece/nephew labels depend on context. Túró Rudi and túró are not accurately described simply as ricotta. Tejszín is cream; the picture's English whipped cream gloss is approximate. Source ól = horse stable is imprecise. Country names and prices are retained as historical learning content.

`Learning in Spanish.docx` includes artificial or doubtful forms (for example mitt, hívvám and tűdő), approximate sound comparisons and explicitly invented syllables. These are retained as evidence and are not promoted as dictionary headwords. The source claim that ki is always singular is too broad: the later plural sheet explicitly has kik/mik. A gloss of szerettek as they loved is ambiguous without context; do not infer a past-tense lesson from it. Malformed learner strings such as Magyarországonban or Zürichbenül are not paradigms.

## Image extraction

- The canonical grammar reference reconstructs the main learned paradigms. Raw OCR is not a reconstructed table and must not be used as one. The original image and bounding boxes are supplied for checking.
- Handwritten timetable I0327: activities and time ranges are readable, but weekday-to-cell alignment is not fully certain in the compact preview. No guessed weekday assignments or exercise answers were entered.
- Maps and route diagrams rely on layout. Their original bytes are included in both packages; OCR alone cannot establish route geometry.
- Decorative/object photographs were inspected and described. A pictured object is not treated as a newly taught word unless its label or an accompanying lesson attests it.
- A complete word-by-word proofreading of all 6,545 OCR lines remains outstanding. Vocabulary coverage is broad but cannot be certified exhaustive while image OCR and audio speech remain unresolved. The exact source text and all image evidence are retained to make further review possible.

## Audio/video

No speech-recognition tool was available in this session. Do not treat media metadata or written listening questions as audio transcripts. This is an explicit remaining coverage gap, not evidence that the recordings contain no additional vocabulary.

## Inference boundaries

Dictionary English glosses supplied for labelled images and Spanish examples are editorial translations. POS labels inferred from ordinary use are study metadata; polysemy and context may require refinement. Automated occurrence matching can find incidental mentions and uncorrected learner forms. Earliest encounter is reported separately within each dated sequence; subsequent encounters do not by themselves prove an expansion. Only explicit tables/explanations and specified text discussions support the grammar-teaching evidence.
